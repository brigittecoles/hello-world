The V-Seal

A stigmergic key — one mark that is a glyph, a cryptographic seed, and its own verification rule — hidden in the artwork of a credential and its certificate.

The V-Seal welds provenance into the mark itself. Instead of stapling a signature onto a file, it scatters a secret across hundreds of sub-pixel traces in the artwork — invisible until an agent re-walks the field with the right seed, at which point a V emerges and the mark proves it is authentic and unaltered. This guide documents the method and gives exact instructions to reproduce, verify, and operate it.

01What the V-Seal is

One object · three roles

The V-Seal is a single construct that plays three roles simultaneously. It is not three features bolted together — the same 256-bit value drives all three, so they cannot disagree.

The three roles of one seal
1
GlyphA V that is never drawn on the face. It emerges from the existing dot-field only when the mark is read with the correct seed — a covert constellation.
2
Seed / vectorA 256-bit key that deterministically generates both the V's exact geometry and a fractional-pixel nudge on every dot. The V you eventually see is, in effect, a visual hash of the seed.
3
Verification ruleRegenerate the whole field and the V from the seed and compare to what is in the file. The glyph is an executable authenticity check, not decoration.

Two profiles share the method. The coin credential (vseal-1) binds the seal to the registry number and the artwork. The certificate — the Bill of Assessment (vseal-cert-1) — additionally binds it to the finding, so altering any printed score breaks the seal.

02Why stigmergy

Coordination through traces in a shared medium

Stigmergy is how ants coordinate without messaging each other: each deposits and reads pheromone in the environment, and the environment itself carries the memory. A stigmergic key applies that idea to a mark. The key is never stored as one value anywhere. It is deposited as hundreds of faint traces — tiny per-dot offsets — spread across the whole artwork, densest along the V. An agent reassembles the key only by re-walking the field with the seed.

Disturb the medium — move, add, or drop a single dot — and the traces desynchronise. The key is therefore also the tamper-evidence.

This is why the coin and the certificate are ideal hosts. The coin is already a field of hundreds of ASCII dots; the certificate is given a guilloché microprint field that reads as genuine security printing. In both, the "pheromone substrate" is native to the design, so the key hides in plain sight.

03Architecture at a glance

From master secret to a self-verifying mark

The pipeline is deterministic end to end. The same inputs always produce the same seal, and verification is simply the pipeline run forward again and compared.

Deterministic pipeline. The coin stops at K; the certificate folds the finding in to make Kf.

04The cryptographic construction

Definitions and derivations

Everything below uses SHA-256 and HMAC-SHA-256 only, so it is reproducible in any language (the reference build uses Python hashlib/hmac for encoding and browser WebCrypto for verification).

Why HMAC twice

K gives every token an independent seed from one master. Folding content_hash into Kf means the physical dot-field is a function of both the seed and the assessment — so a changed score doesn't just fail a text check, it desynchronises every trace.

05Carriers & embedding

Where the key lives, and how

The key is embedded as a sub-pixel offset on each dot: two bits of the keystream choose the sign of a ±DELTA (0.09 px) nudge in x and y. At 0.09 px the shift is invisible at any zoom, but written at three-decimal precision it reads back exactly. Two carriers:

  • Coin credential — the mark's own ASCII dot-field (≈385 circles) is the substrate. No extra ornament is added; the key rides the art you already approved.
  • Certificate — a dedicated guilloché microprint field (≈913 microdots on a fine grid) plus a microtext hairline, placed at the foot of the Bill. It reads as genuine anti-forgery printing, gives ample capacity, and is where the V emerges; the assay medallion is set into its right end and lights on a pass.
0.09px
The per-dot nudge. Invisible on screen and in print, exact on read-back at three decimals — the whole key is a field of these.

Both marks are drawn from vector shapes, never fonts, so they render identically in any wallet, browser, or print pipeline — a hard lesson from an earlier font-dependent version that collapsed when fonts were substituted. The commitment and, for the certificate, the full finding are embedded in a <metadata> block the verifier reads:

metadata block (certificate)
<metadata id="vseal">{
  "scheme":"vseal-cert-1", "registry":"SAS-2026-0007",
  "content_hash":"e16764c1…", "commit":"5310f561…",
  "dots":913, "delta":0.09,
  "grid":{…}, "vbase":{…}, "finding":{…}
}</metadata>

06The verification rule

Three checks, all must hold

Verification re-derives the key from the seed inputs and runs the pipeline forward, comparing against the file. It is exact-offset: each dot's measured offset must equal the expected ±DELTA within a tight epsilon (0.03 px), so any move — not just a sign flip — fails.

  • Field — every trace reproduces from the key (coin: 385/385; certificate: 913/913).
  • Commitment — SHA-256(key) equals the committed C (in the file, and ideally on-chain).
  • Content (certificate) — every printed level matches the sealed finding, catching a forger who edits only the visible text.

These rows are the actual self-test outcomes for the certificate. On a pass, the verifier lights the V along its stigmergic trail and activates the medallion; on a fail, it flashes the desynced dots.

07Security model & threats

What it guarantees, and what it does not

  • Keyed, not obscure. Security rests on the 256-bit seed, not on hiding the method. Without the key you cannot reproduce a single correct offset.
  • Tamper-evident. Any moved, added, or removed dot desynchronises the field. Content binding extends this to the printed data.
  • The commitment must be anchored externally. The <metadata> commitment only proves the file is self-consistent. A forger can mint their own key and embed a matching commitment. Trust comes from comparing C to the value recorded on-chain at issuance — the anchor a forger cannot forge.
  • Not encryption. This authenticates and watermarks; it does not conceal a readable message. It is a keyed steganographic MAC over the artwork.
Always verify against the chain, not just the file. The seal proves the art is the one bound to a key; the chain proves the key is the one you issued.

08Robustness & tuning

Parameters and their trade-offs

  • DELTA (0.09 px) — the nudge magnitude. Larger survives coarser re-encoding but risks visibility; smaller is safer visually but more fragile. Keep well below cell spacing.
  • Decimal precision (3) — coordinates must be written with enough decimals to preserve DELTA on read-back. Two decimals is the floor; one decimal destroys the signal.
  • Dot count — more dots = more capacity and a crisper V. Coin ≈385, certificate ≈913.
  • SVGO / optimizers — aggressive coordinate rounding to one decimal erases the field, which correctly reads as "seal broken." Keep ≥2-decimal precision in the pipeline, or treat the on-chain commitment as the durable proof and the field as the bound-to-art layer.
  • Optional hardening — add Reed–Solomon/repetition coding across the field to tolerate light re-encoding, or raise DELTA to ~0.2 px where print tolerances demand it.

09Instructions (step by step)

Encode · verify · self-test · anchor · customise

A · Seal a coin credential

# master secret + registry no.  → writes sas-coin-silver-keyed.svg
python3 vseal.py encode "AINIA-master-demo" "SAS-2026-0007"
# prints: seedK, commitment C, dot count, glyph-V count

B · Seal a certificate (Bill of Assessment)

# edit the finding F at the top of vseal_cert.py, then:
python3 vseal_cert.py encode "AINIA-master-demo"   # → sas-bill-sealed.svg
# prints: K, Kf, commit, content_hash, dots

C · Verify

# command line
python3 vseal.py       verify sas-coin-silver-keyed.svg <seedK-hex>
python3 vseal_cert.py  verify sas-bill-sealed.svg "AINIA-master-demo"

# in a browser (offline, WebCrypto) — open and enter the passphrase:
sas-vseal-verifier.html      # coin: V emerges, 385/385
sas-cert-verifier.html       # certificate: 3 checks, V + medallion

D · Self-test (proves the guarantees)

python3 vseal.py      selftest   # authentic PASS · tamper FAIL · wrong-seed FAIL
python3 vseal_cert.py selftest   # + edited-score FAIL · altered-finding FAIL

E · Anchor on-chain

  • At mint, record the commitment C (and, for a certificate, content_hash) in the token's tokenURI or a registry mapping keyed by registry number.
  • To verify later, recompute C from the seed and compare it to the on-chain value — not only to the copy inside the file.
  • Store the artwork on-chain via SSTORE2 chunks for a fully self-contained register, or anchor the keccak-256 hash on-chain with the SVG on Arweave/IPFS to cut mint gas.

F · Swap in the real AINIA V

The reference build uses a seed-derived placeholder V. To bind your actual glyph, change the geometry function in one place per profile:

# coin: vseal.py → v_geometry() / VBASE
# cert: vseal_cert.py → v_geometry() / VBASE  (apexx, apexy, topy, span)
  • For a line-based V, set the two stroke segments' endpoints from your glyph and keep the seed jitter (or drop it for a fixed shape).
  • For an arbitrary glyph, replace the "distance-to-segments" membership test with a point-in-path test against your SVG path; the field mechanics are unchanged.
  • Keep the same geometry in the browser verifier's vgeom() so the reveal matches the encoder.

11Key management & operations

Running it safely

  • Master secret custody. The master secret is the root of every seal. Hold it in an HSM or secrets manager; the seals in files never contain it.
  • Per-token seeds. K (and Kf) are derived, not stored — regenerate them on demand from the master + registry (+ finding).
  • Public verification. To let third parties verify without the master, publish the per-token seed K/Kf as a "verification vector," or run verification as a service; the on-chain C stays the anchor either way.
  • Revocation. Because the credential is soulbound and is the registry entry, revocation is a burn or an on-chain flag by the issuer — the seal itself does not expire.
  • Re-issuance. A corrected finding is a new certificate with a new content_hash → new Kf → new seal, linked to the prior one by registry number.

12Caveats

Read before production

This is a design and provenance prototype for watermarking your own artwork — not a substitute for a smart-contract audit or a formal cryptographic review before production use. The scheme authenticates and binds; it does not encrypt. Its guarantees hold only when the commitment is anchored outside the file (on-chain) and the master secret is kept secret. Coordinate-rounding optimizers will invalidate a seal by design; keep the artwork on a controlled pipeline or rely on the on-chain commitment as the durable proof.
